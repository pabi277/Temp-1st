# Niche validation method

Use this loop before coding. A star rating, update date, or complaint is a
signal to inspect—not proof of demand, abandonment, or revenue.

## The seven-step loop

1. **Seed from the owner's life.** List real exams, local language knowledge,
   work, hobbies, and problems the owner can verify. Do not choose a niche only
   because an agent suggests it.
2. **Search the exact user phrase.** Search Play for the phrase a real user
   would type. Record country, language, date, query, and listing links. Open
   the top five relevant results, not just the first one.
3. **Score the five.** Record stars and last-update date. A 3.5–4.2 rating can
   signal unsatisfied demand; a listing last updated more than a year ago can
   signal stale maintenance while still ranking. Read ten 1-star reviews per
   listing where available. Turn recurring, verified complaints into a free
   product spec. These are heuristics, not guarantees.
4. **Classify the market.** Crowded-but-bad can be an opportunity; an empty
   search can mean no demand; a giant-owned category may be hard to enter.
   Confirm the interpretation with evidence before deciding.
5. **Find the niche inside the niche.** Choose a language, state, or audience
   the owner can serve with uncopyable care: source-checked local detail,
   excellent language quality, and fast, respectful review replies.
6. **Check incumbent ad friction.** Install and use candidate apps. Note where
   ads appear and whether they interrupt the task. A store-page “contains ads”
   label does not prove ad-poison. Differentiate with respectful placements.
7. **Write the verdict line.** Record: demand / staleness / complaints / owner
   advantage. If evidence is missing, the verdict is “validate further,” not
   “build now.”

## Worked example: RTO learner-licence practice

This is a small, dated evidence peek to demonstrate the loop—not a complete
market audit or a recommendation to build. Search results and reviews change.
The owner must redo the top-five inspection in the intended country and
language before deciding.

1. **Seed:** the candidate owner has real access to learners and can personally
   verify learner-licence questions for one state and language. If that is not
   true, there is no owner advantage yet.
2. **Exact phrase:** try the phrase a learner would actually type, such as
   `RTO learner licence test [STATE] [LANGUAGE]`. Replace the brackets with the
   chosen state/language; save the exact query and date.
3. **Top five:** open five relevant Play listings and record rating, update
   date, and ten 1-star complaint themes per listing. One listing visible on
   the Play store page for “RTO Exam Bangla: Licence Test” showed 5.0 stars,
   about 2.9K reviews, 100K+ downloads, and an update date of 26 August 2026
   when checked on 10 October 2026. That is a fresh, high-rated competitor—not
   evidence of abandonment or a 3.5–4.2 dissatisfaction signal. One visible
   review mentioned a Bengali spelling error; one comment is not a ten-review
   spec. [Open the listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rtoexam.bangla&hl=en).
4. **Classify:** this one listing suggests people use the category, but it does
   not prove the exact query's demand or show that the market is “crowded but
   bad.” Inspect the other four results and their 1-star reviews first.
5. **Niche inside the niche:** a possible edge is state-specific practice in a
   language the owner can quality-check against current official material.
   Verify each answer with an authoritative source; do not copy a competitor's
   question bank.
6. **Ad friction:** the listing says it contains ads, but that does not reveal
   placement quality. Install it and observe the real flow. Do not claim
   “ad-poison” without direct evidence. A new app can still commit to
   optional rewarded ads and no surprise interruptions.
7. **Verdict line:** `Demand: category has one visible 100K+ download listing,
   exact-query demand still unmeasured; staleness: sampled listing updated
   2026-08-26; complaints: one spelling complaint surfaced, ten 1-star reviews
   not yet audited; owner advantage: possible only if state/language content is
   source-checked by the owner. Decision: validate the other four listings and
   reviews before code.`

The worked listing is a public store result, not an endorsement. Its description
and data-safety answers are developer-provided; verify all facts independently.
